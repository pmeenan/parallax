## Dimensions and viewing

From the [kit spec](../../../reference/d1-courtyard-kit.md#assembly-and-pieces), the
[approved blockout](../../../reference/concept-art-batch-188-blockout-v2.md) and the K1 house:
- **Pitch 40°.** Walls 6 m, ridges about 9 m above the floor. On the K1 house (12.25 × 6.25 m, plates
  topping out at 6.5 m) the ridge lands near 9.2 m.
- **Gable roof.** Eaves along the 12.25 m front and back, gables on the 6.25 m sides, as in KIT-008 A.
  The hip in KIT-008 B is later coverage.
- **Projections.** Eave about 0.45 m horizontal beyond the wall face, verge about 0.25–0.3 m beyond
  the gable face.
- **Tiles.** Roman pan and cover tiles about 0.40 m long and 24 mm thick. Pans taper 0.25 to 0.19 m
  and are curved troughs; covers taper 0.18 to 0.12 m and are near half-round. Each tile's narrow end,
  with its thickness, nests inside the next tile's wide end. Seven channels per 2 m bay (0.286 m
  pitch); about 0.30 m of each tile is exposed. Ridge caps about 0.45 m long and 0.40 m wide, bedded in
  lime mortar. (Revised 2026-09-27 from 0.45 m tiles at a 0.22 m pitch: at that pitch the covers
  hid the pans, and MAT-011's proportions show the pans' troughs between the covers.)
- **Structure seen from below.** Eave board on joist or rafter tails at about 0.5–0.7 m spacing, with
  end grain; verge (barge) boards; gable framing in the K1 oak with plaster infill.
- **Kit pieces** ([kit spec](../../../reference/d1-courtyard-kit.md#assembly-and-pieces): 2 m
  along the eave, pivot at the eave midpoint):
  - eave-to-ridge slope bays, one per 2 m wall bay;
  - ridge sections;
  - left and right verge ends;
  - gable infill.

  This house's run from eave to ridge is about 3.6 m of slope. A deeper or shallower house is a
  new slope variant generated by the same script.
- **Viewing.** At 1.7 m eye height the eave is 5 m up. The roof is read mostly from 5–30 m, and
  from underneath at the eave. The closest view is about 1 m, from raised streets and stairs,
  matching MAT-011's framing.

## Reference breakdown (the defect checklist)

| Scale | Traits to reproduce |
| --- | --- |
| Layout (m) | Gable roof over the K1 walls; the ridge centred and level; the eave line straight along the facade; verges overhanging each gable; the gable triangle closed with framing and plaster |
| Form (cm) | Pan and cover channels in regular courses, with small irregular offsets and tilts; courses slightly bellied between supports; covers seated over the pan edges; ridge caps overlapping along the ridge on a mortar bed; tails projecting evenly under an eave board |
| Edge (cm–mm) | Solid clay edges showing the fired body at mouths, verges and cap ends; chipped and worn tile edges; a few cracked or replaced tiles; oak tails with end grain and checks |
| Surface (mm) | Clay grain, fine pits and sand inclusions; slightly uneven moulded curvature; lichen rosettes and fine speckle |
| Colour | Red-orange to brown kiln variation per tile, a few pale and darker tiles; pale weathering on up-facing crowns; darker, damper laps and channels; restrained moss in laps and at the eave; soot-free |
| Contact | Tiles bear on each other and on the eave board with no floating tile or visible interpenetration; the roof sits on the head plates with no gap; the ridge seals both slopes; the verge covers the tile ends at the gables |
| Drainage | Uphill over downhill everywhere; pan channels open to the eave mouths; no gap through to the roof void or sky |
| Lighting | No lighting in albedo; relief reads under the calibrated side sun and survives overcast |

## Question

Can the script-first method build a tiled roof at the references' quality over a large area? The
roof has thousands of repeated units, so it tests the representation more than the walls did:
- **Default:** every tile is real curved geometry, drawn from an atlas of about two dozen unique
  tile textures at about 0.75 mm per texel. Per-tile placement jitter, per-tile kiln tint, and a
  per-piece low-frequency weathering overlay (lichen, moss, damp) add variation.
- **The `street` and `overview` views decide** whether that variation is enough to hide the atlas
  repetition.

Record where the wall method had to change: large repeated units, per-instance variation,
and overlapping thin shells.

## Must-fix defects

1. A stamped or perfectly regular tile field: identical tiles, identical rows, no kiln variation.
2. Tiles that cannot drain:
   - a downhill tile over an uphill one;
   - sky or the roof void visible between tiles;
   - floating tiles or visibly interpenetrating courses.
3. Plastic or uniform terracotta: no clay grain, no fired edge body, sharp CG edges.
4. Unsupported eaves or ridge: a tile overhang with no board or tails, or ridge caps floating or
   unbedded.
5. Open gables or verges: the roof void visible, gable infill missing, or tile ends uncovered.
6. A moss carpet, soot, or lighting painted into albedo.
7. A gap or interpenetration between the roof and the K1 walls, or new timber that does not match
   the K1 oak.
8. A wavy or broken ridge or eave line. A slight sag of a few centimetres is allowed.

## Allowance and end

- **Time box:** one to two work sessions for the preview loop and the final build. Scripted
  previews inside it are free; full-quality candidate handoffs count, two by default (D-196).
  Under D-207 the quality gate, not the estimate, ends the work.
- **Ends with:** the human's artistic decision on the source. If accepted, the K2 delivery
  package follows: LODs, maps, roof pieces in the library, and the roof on the installed test house.
- **Out of scope:**
  - the hip roof and hip ridge;
  - chimneys, dormers, gutters and downpipes (none in the references);
  - interiors (D-208);
  - collision;
  - runtime delivery, QA admission and the courtyard assembly (A1).

  The test house has no rear wall. Cameras avoid the back of the house, and the roof's rear slope
  is judged from the overview only.
