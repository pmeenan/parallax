## Verdict: revise once; do not replicate this candidate

The new faces avoid the obvious repeated pale mineral islands in the restored
family. The four outlines and aspect ratios also provide useful variation.
However, their material now reads as soft cloudy gray-white slabs, losing the
cream limestone identity that the brief requires. That is a substantive
regression, even though repetition is reduced. The final allowed cycle should
restore the reference's material hierarchy while retaining independent patterns.

## Required corrections for the final cycle

1. **Replace the all-over cloud structure with limestone structure.** In unlit
   and walking views, each face has a similar density of soft gray/white clouds
   across almost its entire area. MAT-001 has quieter weathered gray fields,
   irregular cream/tan mineral boundaries, smaller fractured inclusions and
   occasional distinct pits. KIT-001 distributes these features unequally
   between stones. Preserve broad independent fields, but give their boundaries
   firmer, irregular detail and leave deliberate quiet regions. Do not merely
   raise contrast on the current cloud texture: that would make camouflage
   patches more pronounced.
2. **Restore the warmer, wider material palette.** Textured and opposing-light
   views are predominantly chalk white and cool gray. The source reference has
   cream, muted tan and weathered neutral gray in a less washed-out balance.
   The unlit result confirms that the issue is not only the beauty light.
   Match the material palette before altering lighting; vary the proportions
   modestly among faces without introducing unrelated stone colors.
3. **Restore sparse physical surface accents.** Gray shows almost blank flat
   tops, and grazing shows gentle undulation without the selective small pits,
   chipped mineral boundaries and worn shallow relief visible in MAT-001.
   Some quiet flat areas are desirable; the entire face should not be equally
   smooth. Add a restrained, physically sized population of shallow irregular
   pits and limited rough patches, with several visibly quieter areas. Do not
   recover detail by applying uniform high-frequency noise everywhere.
4. **Remove the thin rolled-edge impression visible at grazing.** The front
   edges read as soft rounded sheets, and the front-right stone has a dark
   horizontal recess beneath a pale projecting edge near the center seam.
   This does not read as the worn, chipped solid limestone section in MAT-001.
   Check the actual side profile and local ground contact before changing
   shadows. Keep edge damage selective rather than forming a continuous lip.

The earth in this sample also appears substantially smoother and less granular
than `restored-family/oblique.png`. Because the brief calls earth/contact a
control, preserve that control in the next comparative capture or explicitly
identify the sample-ground difference. This screen does not grant contact or
whole-patch acceptance.

## What to preserve and how to judge the revision

Keep the broad actual-size faces and distinguishable outlines; no obvious
identical large mineral landmark is visible across this four-stone sample.
Fine detail is no longer obviously magnified into the former large islands.
These are useful advances, but absence of a repeated landmark is not sufficient
when all stones share the same cloudy texture character.

Reinspect the same seven views after the final change, with special emphasis
on walking/unlit for mineral identity and gray/grazing for selective relief and
side contact. A supported outcome should remain recognizably in MAT-001's
cream-limestone family at walking scale while showing independent broad
patterns across the four pieces. If that does not survive the second cycle,
reject this material method and retain the previous source as the control;
do not silently add a third cycle or propagate the family into the patch.
