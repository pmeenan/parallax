Human continuation follows the soil-to-stone edge approval and an explanation of
possible lighting embedded in the generated color image. Preserve the approved
contact, shape and restored material. Do not interpret dark mineral color alone as
a defect or demand a flat-looking unlit image as proof of correct reflectance.

Question: does fixed surface shading visibly contradict moving illumination at
close and walking distance? Selected KIT-002 batch104 and MAT-001 batch108 remain
the stone targets. Start from restored-family/source.blend, using Blender 5.2.1.

One diagnostic package, 20 active / 30 elapsed minutes. Capture eight light azimuths
at fixed elevation, energy and camera for close and walking views; four corresponding
gray controls retain geometry, bump and roughness while removing only base color.
Keep images/settings/provenance and an editable diagnostic scene. Do not modify the
original scene or the live unsaved Blender session. Two independent screens and lead
inspect actual frames before any dependent material change.

Outcome must distinguish observed lighting contradictions from suspicious static
texture detail. If no material contradiction is demonstrated, retain the limitation
as unproven and advance the practical proof rather than erase useful stone character.
If one is demonstrated, name the affected scale and features, then scope a separate
bounded correction. More depth is not presumed necessary; current geometry already
uses height-derived displacement. This package does not grant runtime or full-art QA.

Source inspection: the input prompt deliberately assigns different mineral colors to
raised and eroded areas; the retained material also multiplies color by a ramp driven
by the smoothed height field. Therefore relief-like contrast in unlit color alone
cannot distinguish intended correlated mineral color from unwanted illumination.
The diagnosis must identify a concrete directional conflict in the lit sequence.
