Outcome: conditional visual pass for source-only transfer evidence and proceeding to a 4 m repetition study. No gross transfer loss warrants blocking that study. This is not full asset QA or artistic acceptance.

Inspected a/b/c comparison sheets, including native-low, then opened each member's high-source, reimport, high-source-opposing and reimport-opposing PNGs at native image scale. The comparison is against the high source, not merely agreement between two low-mesh renders.

- All three retain their distinct broad mineral landmarks, warm cream/dark gray family palette and recognizable limestone identity. No broad color-space washout, missing texture, obvious UV seam or new family mismatch appears.
- Reimport silhouettes are slightly simpler: small edge chips and jagged transitions become smoother or straighter, particularly at near corners and long outer edges. The main shape, thickness, notches and worn contour survive. No return of the rejected broad ribbed shoulder is visible.
- Close inspection shows reduced fine relief and slightly softer/smoother side-face detail relative to the high source. Some corner facets become more readable. These are real simplification losses; the transferred mesh is not visually identical to the high source.
- Opposing light still changes the illuminated faces and shading as expected. I see no large flipped-normal patch or inverted overall relief. C's reimport-opposing has a small dark angular mark on the cream patch just behind the near long edge, around image x860/y720, less apparent in its high source. Record as a close-view residual, not a demonstrated study-blocking defect.
- Retained mineral character remains consistent with the selected stone direction. This transfer does not itself close the remaining difference between the source and the sharper natural fracture detail of the reference.

Conditions: retain high-source comparisons as the fidelity baseline and judge repetition in the actual patch, rather than infer it from these isolated stones.
