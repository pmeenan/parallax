# Broad family cycle 1 — independent quality screen

Actual images inspected: `broad-family/candidate1-corrected/` oblique,
opposing-light, walking, reverse, grazing, gray and unlit; Batch103 KIT-001
assembly and Batch108 MAT-001 limestone originals. Also inspected the approved
`restored-family/oblique.png` material/contact control. The earlier `candidate1/`
precision preflight is excluded from this artistic screen. Read the current
broad-family brief, paving follow-up and asset production instructions.

**Result: material identity and contact remain blockers.** The family has broad
faces at varied dimensions, but removing recognizable old landmarks has also
removed most of the limestone's characteristic surface hierarchy. This is a
quality assessment, not human artistic acceptance or a delivery/engine verdict.

## Findings

- **Material identity — blocker.** Oblique, reverse and walking views read as
  pale cool-gray slabs covered with soft cloud mottling. MAT-001 has connected
  warm-gray mineral fields, cream/buff transitions, localized pits and sharper
  worn breaks. Here the pattern is fuzzy and similarly active over nearly every
  face. The same cloud pattern dominates unlit, so changing the key light alone
  will not recover the missing identity. Opposing light changes edge shadows
  convincingly; I see no concrete directional color/light conflict requiring a
  separate reflectance rewrite.
- **Physical detail scale — partly improved, materially incomplete.** No
  magnified pore islands or coarse grain jump out at walking distance. However,
  gray shows nearly blank flat interiors with slight uniform texture; grazing
  exposes soft rolled edges with little localized stone fracture. Fine detail
  has become too subordinate to establish limestone. Preserve the calm faces,
  but restore a few shallow pits and broken mineral transitions at explicit
  physical scale instead of increasing noise across the whole face. The receipt's
  physical scales are authoring metadata, not an image-based calibration.
- **Broad repetition — partial success.** I cannot identify an exact repeated
  landmark among the four pieces. Their differing dimensions help. Nevertheless,
  the same cloud size, tonal range and even coverage make all four look like
  samples of one procedural fill. Independent seeds have not yet produced
  distinct large-scale organizations. This four-piece sample cannot establish
  a nonrepeating installed patch.
- **Contact/edge geometry — blocker.** Grazing clearly shows a dark horizontal
  ledge below the front-right stone's top, terminating in an abrupt vertical step,
  plus undercut-looking dark lengths on other exposed edges. They read as thin
  caps over sidewalls. Oblique/opposing views retain black cutout-like edge
  lengths and smooth empty soil channels. The approved control's embedded grit
  and irregular soil overlap are visibly absent here. No stone-to-stone top
  intersection is evident, but these images do not prove watertight geometry or
  triangle-level contact; the AABB clearance is not a substitute.

## Direction for the remaining cycle

Keep the actual broad dimensions and independent silhouettes. Replace the even
cloud distribution with differently placed connected mineral regions and quieter
worn zones; restore restrained cream/buff versus warm-gray color separation and
localized pits/chipped facets without reusing the old recognizable islands.
Prioritize one convincing complete face over merely adding fine grain. Repair the
visible cap/sidewall ledges, and use the accepted gritty, partly buried contact
control around the new outlines. Reinspect the same seven states before any broad
placement or transfer claim. If identity remains generic at the two-cycle boundary,
record the method as unresolved/rejected rather than treating absence of exact
repeats as success.
