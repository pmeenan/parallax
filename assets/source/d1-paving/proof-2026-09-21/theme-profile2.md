# Independent profile-contact2 theme screen

Inspected all five actual profile-contact2 PNGs: oblique, reverse-light, walking,
overcast and joint-detail. Compared with profile-contact1, family-contact2 and the
previously inspected MAT-018 target. No asset edits made.

**Final-cycle verdict: reject profile-contact2 as the contact solution. Do not
expand this result to a full patch on the strength of this screen.** The farther
inward probe raises the earth locally but reintroduces angular soil incursions into
low bevels: most visibly the front stone's right edge and small front-edge notch,
and the long stone's near-left edge. These are visible in walking and overcast as
well as oblique, so they are not confined to an extreme close-up.

The long dark band along the left stone in joint-detail also persists. It still
reads as a continuous recessed contact boundary instead of MAT-018's broken,
irregularly packed seam. The lit image alone cannot distinguish exposed shadowed
stone sidewall from an actual geometric void. A same-geometry neutral/semantic
diagnostic is appropriate before diagnosing that band's physical cause; do not
treat darkness alone as proof of floating geometry.

Compared with profile1, this is a worse silhouette/contact tradeoff without a
decisive visual seam improvement. Profile1 or family-contact2 remains a preferable
provisional small-sample presentation with its documented contact limitations.
Edge aggregate still tends toward a narrow perimeter necklace and the channel
floor remains less richly packed than the target. Mineral family identity survives.

The two-cycle package ends with the natural-contact criterion unmet. Preserve the
failed result and diagnostic evidence. Any later implementation should be separately
bounded around the diagnosed cause rather than another unverified height increase.
Human artistic acceptance, full-patch repetition, rights/QA/library admission and
installed runtime validation remain open.

## Same-geometry diagnostic addendum

Subsequently inspected `diagnostic-gray.png` and `diagnostic-regions.png`, with gray
identifying stone, green fitted soil, orange grit and brown base earth. These change
the physical diagnosis: the long dark band belongs predominantly to an exposed,
shadowed stone sidewall. Green fitted soil meets its lower visible boundary; there
is no visible intervening base-earth strip demonstrating a continuous open trench
there. The neutral view exposes a smooth, fairly uniform sidewall band and a thin
ledge. Raising soil merely to erase that shaded face is the wrong remedy.

Physical contact in this inspected seam is therefore supported by the diagnostic,
while the earlier lit appearance remains unconvincing. The regions also confirm
local green incursions into the long stone's low bevel at the upper-left of the
image, so the profile2 clipping regression remains real. Retain the rejection of
profile2's height choice; preserve profile1/family-contact2 as provisional alternatives.
The next diagnosed question is the uniform sidewall/ledge shape and its shaded
material appearance, plus locally safe soil contact, not filling an assumed void.
This diagnosis does not prove contact everywhere or grant full-patch acceptance.
